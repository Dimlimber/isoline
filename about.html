About

The name, the method, and what we will never do with your answers.

The name

An isoline is a line on a map joining points of equal value. On a topographic map they are contour lines; every point on one line is at the same elevation. Isoline puts every marketing organization on one such map. Your report tells you which line you are on. The Map tells the industry what the terrain looks like.

What the Index measures

Six dimensions of AI maturity in the marketing function: Ambition, Data, Stack, Practice, People and Guardrails. Each is scored from 0 to 100 from evidence of practice: what is deployed, how often, by whom, with what data, measured how. Self-declared ambition counts for little on its own. The overall elevation is a weighted composite; the weights are published with the first report and held constant thereafter, so a score in one year means the same thing in the next.

Four bands describe the terrain: Lowland (0 to 25), Foothills (26 to 50), Highlands (51 to 75) and Summit (76 to 100).

Who answers

One fee covers the company. The survey link can go to anyone in the marketing organization, and the more people who answer, the better the profile: brand, performance, lifecycle and CRM, content, analytics, marketing operations. Each respondent answers for the jobs they own. Where respondents disagree, the report says so and shows a confidence indicator rather than averaging the disagreement away.

Peer sets

Every company is placed against three sets: its own industry, all industries, and the top decile of the whole panel. An industry peer set is only shown once it contains at least eight companies; until then the report shows the all-industry placement and tells you how many more companies your industry needs. No peer is ever named.

What vendors see, and what they never see

Technology vendors subscribe to the Isoline Map per demand space. They see aggregates: share of each space by industry and maturity band, the competitor set, satisfaction, switching intent and budget direction, refreshed as the panel re-surveys. Every cell has at least eight companies in it. Vendors never see a company’s name, its individual answers, or any cut small enough to infer them. This is written into the participant terms and into every vendor contract.

Who pays

Participants pay to take the Index. Vendors pay to see the Map. Readers pay for the reports. No platform sponsors the study and no vendor underwrites a cohort. That is what makes the map worth reading from either side.

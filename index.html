Why now

The old maturity index measured digital. The question has changed.

For a decade, marketing leaders had one benchmark to answer “how far along are we?”: a consultancy survey, sponsored by a platform, run every two or three years, free to join and never followed up. Its last edition, in 2024, found average maturity had fallen since 2021.

AI has since rewritten every job in the function, from insight to content to media to lifecycle. Nobody measures how it is actually being used from the inside, on a panel large enough to compare against your own industry, every year.

Isoline is that panel. Paid by participants, so it answers to them. Annual, so movement shows. Vendor-neutral, so the map can be trusted by both sides.

Six dimensions

Each scored 0 to 100 from evidence of practice, not self-declared ambition. The overall elevation is a weighted composite.

  • 01AmbitionAI strategy, executive sponsorship, budget commitment
  • 02DataFirst-party data quality, connectedness across channels, consent
  • 03StackTooling in use for each marketing job, integration, AI-native versus bolted on
  • 04PracticeWhere AI is actually deployed across insight, content, media, lifecycle and measurement, and how often
  • 05PeopleSkills, roles, agency versus in-house, ways of working
  • 06GuardrailsGovernance, brand safety, IP, and measurement of AI’s impact

Four bands

0 – 25

Lowland

Pilots and personal tools. AI use depends on individuals, not the organization.

26 – 50

Foothills

AI in a few jobs, usually content and media. Data still lives in channels.

51 – 75

Highlands

AI in most jobs with shared data, clear owners and measured results.

76 – 100

Summit

The function is re-designed around AI. Budget moves in weeks. Guardrails are routine.

The Isoline Map · for technology vendors

Marketing teams don’t buy categories. They hire tools for jobs. The Map shows which.

A demand space is a recurring buying situation: a job the marketing team needs done and the context it arises in. Every company in the panel tells us which tools it uses for which job, how satisfied it is, and where next year’s budget is going.

The map is the same across industries. What differs is how dense each space is, which tools win in it, and how that changes by maturity band. Each vendor is overlaid on the same map alongside its competitors.

Vendors subscribe per demand space and receive the cut refreshed as the panel re-surveys. Company-identifiable data is never sold. Minimum cell size is eight.

Available when the panel reaches 300 companies

Eight demand spaces, version one

A working hypothesis to be tested against the first cohort’s answers. The final map is whatever the data says it is.

Principles

A map is only useful if both sides trust it.

Paid by participants

No platform sponsors the study. No vendor underwrites a cohort. The people who take the survey are the people we answer to.

Anonymous by construction

Company-identifiable answers are never sold or shared. Vendors see aggregates by demand space, industry and band, with a minimum cell of eight. You see your own company and a field.

Annual, on purpose

The same instrument every year, so a score means the same thing in 2028 as in 2026 and movement is real movement.
